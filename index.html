How long do I have to file a personal injury claim in North Sydney?

You should get legal advice urgently if you want to claim compensation. The most common claim in a personal injury case is negligence, and under NSW law the limitation period for this is generally 3 years.

This means court proceedings must usually be issued within 3 years of the date you first became aware of your injury. If you want to take legal action to claim compensation for a personal injury, you'll need advice from a solicitor who specialises in these types of cases as soon as possible — some claim types carry shorter notice periods.

Personal Injury Frequently Asked Questions

How to make a claim?

Contact our team for a free case evaluation. We'll review the circumstances of your injury, explain your options plainly, and only proceed if we believe you have a genuine claim.

What is a personal injury claim?

A personal injury claim is a legal process for recovering compensation when you've been hurt due to someone else's negligence — for example a car accident, workplace incident, or public liability matter.

Who can file an injury claim?

Generally anyone injured due to another party's negligence in NSW can file a claim, subject to limitation periods. A family member or legal guardian can file on behalf of a minor or someone unable to act for themselves.

How much is my case worth?

Case value depends on factors like medical costs, lost income, ongoing care needs, and pain and suffering. We provide a realistic estimate after reviewing your circumstances at no cost.

How are damages calculated?

Damages typically account for medical expenses, lost wages, future earning capacity, rehabilitation, and non-economic loss such as pain and suffering.

How long will my case take?

Timelines vary with complexity — straightforward claims may resolve in months, while contested matters can take longer. We'll keep you informed at every stage.

How long will it take to receive compensation?

Once a settlement or judgment is reached, payment timing depends on the insurer or defendant, but is typically processed within a few weeks.
